Pricing proposal · not configured

Recommendation: $3.99/month or $29.99/year in USD. Keep local use free. Annual works out to about $2.50/month and saves about 37% versus twelve monthly payments. These are hypotheses to validate with actual beta users, not measured willingness to pay.

Closest market comparisons

  • Collections Database: US App Store lists Pro $7.99 and Ultra $9.99 purchases; it uses iCloud sync.
  • Tap Forms Pro: iOS only $1.99/month or $12.99/year; macOS+iOS $5.99/month or $49.99/year.
  • Memento Pro: $8/month with 5 GB hosted storage and broader cross-platform features.

Official prices checked November 6, 2026. US App Store and vendor web prices are different sales channels; local checkout can differ.

Lifetime needs a cost decision

Delay lifetime cloud access until storage, backups and support costs are measured. If retained, $149.99 is a starting proposal with an explicit storage cap, not unlimited hosting. That equals roughly five annual payments and does not prove long-term profitability.

Current code grants all Pro products the same 25 GB / 10-device allowance. Consider 5 GB for the launch subscription and a separately costed lifetime allowance. Changing this requires a product decision and implementation; no quota or price was changed by this research.

Position Arca around private personal records, simple native workflows and selective encrypted sync. The current AI feature prepares a prompt for an external assistant; it should not be priced or advertised as an included AI subscription. Measure activation, repeat use, paid conversion, storage and support cost before raising prices.

High-level architecture

iOS: SwiftUI screens and localized String Catalogs → collection/record domain → local encrypted storage and Keychain keys → sync client. Image imports are resized and normalized before encryption. RevenueCat handles Apple purchase information and paywall presentation.

Backend: Laravel on Forge → authenticated device/account APIs, catalog/AI prompt preparation, quota checks and ciphertext transport → PostgreSQL. RevenueCat webhooks and subscriber verification update entitlements. Backend stores ciphertext for encrypted records/images.

End to end: User edits locally → client encrypts permitted data → authenticated upload → second authorized device downloads/decrypts with its Space key. Local-only policy fences and removes cloud copies. An encrypted archive restores into a fresh local Space.
